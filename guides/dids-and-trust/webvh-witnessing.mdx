title: did:webvh witnessing
description: Require witness approvals for changes to a did:webvh, approve changes as a witness agent, and collect and publish the approvals with @credo-ts/webvh.
---

A [did:webvh](./webvh.mdx) can require that witnesses approve every change before it's valid. A witness is another party, identified by a `did:key`, that checks each new log entry and signs an approval. Resolvers reject a published log entry that doesn't have enough approvals.

Two agents take part, and both register `WebVhModule` as described in [Configure](./webvh.mdx#configure):

| Agent | Does |
| --- | --- |
| Controller | Creates and changes the DID, collects the approvals, and publishes the log with its approvals. |
| Witness | Holds a witness key, and approves the log entries that controllers send it. |

Credo signs and checks approvals, but doesn't contact anyone. Your applications send the log from the controller to the witnesses and the approvals back, for example over DIDComm or HTTPS, and the witness application decides which changes it approves.

## Set up a witness

The witness creates an Ed25519 key in its [key management service](../agent-setup/key-management.mdx), and gives the matching `did:key` to the controller:


The witness agent doesn't need a `DidRecord` for the DIDs it witnesses.

## Require witnesses

The controller lists the witnesses, and how many of them must approve a change, in the `witness` option. Then it sends the new log, and the approvals it has so far, to each witness:

```ts showLineNumbers title="controller.ts" webvh-witness.ts section-2
```

`threshold` is the number of witnesses that must approve each new log entry. Pass `witness` in the options of an update to change the witnesses or the threshold.

A `finished` create or update stores the new log entry, but it's not valid until enough witnesses approve it. Don't publish it before then.

## Approve a change

The witness signs the new log entry with its key:

```ts showLineNumbers title="witness.ts" webvh-witness.ts section-3
```

`signWitnessProof` checks that:

- `candidateLog` is `trustedLog` plus exactly one new entry. For the first entry of a DID, `trustedLog` is empty.
- The whole log is valid, and every earlier entry has its approvals in `witnessProofs`.
- The witness key is one of the witnesses that must approve the new entry.

It then signs with the KMS key and returns the approval, which has the `versionId` of the new entry and the signature. Credo doesn't store or fetch anything. When a check fails, `signWitnessProof` throws a `CredoError`, with the reason as its `cause`.

Keep `trustedLog` in your witness application, for example the log you approved last, or the published log. If you take it from the controller's request, the check that the log extends what you approved before means nothing.

Credo checks that the change is valid, not that you should approve it. Inspect the new entry, `candidateLog.at(-1)`, against your own policy before you call `signWitnessProof`.

## Collect and publish approvals

The controller adds the approvals to the ones it has, checks them, and stores them:

```ts showLineNumbers title="controller.ts" webvh-witness.ts section-4
```

`verifyWitnessProofs` returns `verified`, and per log entry the number of approvals and whether there are enough. `rejectedProofs` lists approvals that don't count, with the reason. `storeWitnessProofs` replaces all stored approvals, so keep the approvals of earlier entries.

Then publish `did.jsonl` and `did-witness.json` together, as described in [Publish the DID](./webvh.mdx#publish-the-did).

Every later update and the deactivation go through the same steps. The witness passes the log it approved last as `trustedLog`. Store the approvals before you change the DID again: Credo checks the earlier entries with the stored approvals, or with the published `did-witness.json` if none are stored.

To read the published approvals of a DID without storing them, call `agent.modules.webvh.fetchWitnessProofs(did)`.
